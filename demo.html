The identification problem, live

Wellbeing domains correlate positively everywhere they are measured. Two very different causal stories can explain that: a latent common cause driving every domain, or the domains boosting each other directly with no common cause at all. Below, both processes run live over a simulated population. Watch them settle into the same cross-sectional signature - then use shocks to tell them apart.

§ What to notice

  1. Same signature. Both processes settle to all-positive correlations with one dominant axis. A snapshot of the population cannot tell you which world you are in.
  2. No hidden variable required. Under mutualism, drag coupling to zero and the manifold dissolves. Nothing latent was holding it up - the structure was the coupling.
  3. Shocks discriminate. Under mutualism, a shock to a hub drags the other domains down with it, while a shock to Autonomy barely transmits. Under the latent factor, shocks never propagate between domains at all. Perturbations over time, not snapshots, resolve the question - which is why the planned dissertation work is longitudinal.

method note: illustrative dynamics, not the fitted model. Mutualism mode integrates a generalized Lotka-Volterra system (RK4, multiplicative noise) with qualitative couplings chosen to mirror the asymmetries estimated in the paper - Self-Acceptance most coupling-dependent, Autonomy most self-sustaining - not the Lyapunov-calibrated Jacobian. Latent mode relaxes each domain toward a target set by a person-level factor g with fixed loadings. Correlations are computed on log levels across 120 simulated individuals with heterogeneous capacities. Full treatment: Toward f (PDF).
