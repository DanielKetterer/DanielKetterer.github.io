§1 About

I am a mathematician and software developer in Dayton, Ohio. My background spans systems-level engineering (Linux, C/C++, static analysis), enterprise integration security (OAuth 2.0, Salesforce, Azure), and applied mathematics (optimization, machine learning, control). Much of that work has been on federal systems: legacy Army financial software at Accenture Federal Services, Department of Education data pipelines, and graduate research on autonomous UAV control with the Air Force Research Laboratory. I held a Top Secret clearance for that work; it is currently inactive.

Alongside the engineering, I run an independent research program on the quantitative measurement of human flourishing. The core of it is "f" - a general-factor construct, in the tradition of Spearman's g, modeling flourishing as coupling strength across the domains of a life. The work so far includes a bifactor measurement case across six instrument families, longitudinal economic validation on the MIDUS panel, and a pre-registered analysis plan for the Global Flourishing Study.

What ties the two together is the two-way translation from theory and mathematical structure to code and data structures.

§2 Selected work

2026 – present · Independent research

Toward f: A General Factor of Human Flourishing

Measures of wellbeing - purpose, relationships, competence, self-regard - correlate positively everywhere they are measured. This project asks whether a single general factor, f, underlies that pattern, the way Spearman's g underlies cognitive ability. The paper establishes f as psychometrically coherent across 17 indicators from six independently designed instrument families (ωh = 0.893), shows that it predicts income and employment nine years out on the MIDUS panel beyond demographics and pathology measures, and develops a mutualism dynamical-systems account of what generates the structure. A pre-registered Global Flourishing Study analysis plan and a planned three-paper dissertation extend the work toward policy.
